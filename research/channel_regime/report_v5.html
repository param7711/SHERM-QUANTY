Which line is blue

Settled from the indicator source rather than the screenshot. Of the five Bollinger plots, exactly one is blue — so the gate is the 20-period SMA must lie between the two regression-channel lines.

One number I could not observe. LRC_SH exposes only Length; its deviation multiplier is hard-coded in a script I have not seen. That multiplier is the gate, so it is swept from 0.5 to 3.0 in every test and nothing is fitted to it. At 2.0 the gate is open 87–90% of the time. Send me the LRC_SH source and I will pin it exactly.
02 — The datafour defects, all load-bearing

What was wrong with the bars before anything was tested

Every market-data host is blocked at this session's proxy, so the hourly bars were rebuilt from 1-minute prints in a public archive and resampled to the NSE session — 09:15 to 15:30, seven bars a day, verified at 6.99–7.00 bars per session.

That archive is not clean. Four separate defects had to be fixed before any result was worth printing.

The hole was the dangerous one. A 400-bar regression fitted across it is fitted across a two-year price move that never appeared on any chart, and splits that happened inside the hole never show up as a single-day ratio at all. Before the fix, BANKNIFTY reported a long-side P&L of +22.2 — the entire figure was one fabricated −92% / +1199% pair.

Had I run the backtest on the archive as downloaded, it would have printed a profitable BANKNIFTY strategy built on a price that never traded.

03 — Executionresting orders, not next-bar fills

Giving the strategy its best fair shot

A backtest that signals at one bar's close and fills at the next throws away the snap-back this strategy exists to capture, and nobody trading a band system would do that. So the engine uses resting limit orders at the band levels: the levels for a bar are fixed by the bars before it, so the order is already sitting there when the bar opens.

Fills are taken at the level even when the bar gapped clean through it, which is the conservative side in all four cases. Truncating the price series leaves earlier P&L bit-identical, and every trade reconciles by hand to (exit level − entry level) / entry level − costs.

Entering at the band rather than at the same bar's open is worth about +2.2 Sharpe. That is real, and it is an execution effect, not an edge.

04 — The resultnegative everywhere

Seventeen instruments, no survivors

The gate is not the culprit. It helps — consistently, and slightly: +0.03 to +0.10 Sharpe at channel widths of 1.5 σ and above, +0.25 at 0.5 σ, entirely by trading less. Costs are not the culprit either: at zero cost the mean is still −1.12.

05 — The mechanismwhere the money goes

You are right two times in three, and wrong three times as expensively

This is the whole story, and it is visible in four numbers.

win rate       64.7%        ← right about two trades in three
average win    +0.67%
average loss   −1.97%       ← the losers run three times further
payoff ratio    0.34
───────────────────────────────────────────────
expectancy     −0.26% per trade

A fixed 1.8 σ target against a loss with no stop is a shape, and the shape has a break-even line. At a 64.7% win rate you need a payoff ratio of 0.55. You have 0.34. Put the other way round: at the payoff ratio you actually get, you would need to win 74.7% of trades, not 64.7%.

Every instrument sits below that line, and not marginally.

06 — Monte Carlothree controls, one at a time

Was it the market, or the machinery?

A negative Sharpe says a strategy loses. It does not say why, and the why is the only part that tells you what to build next. There are four places the money can go, and each has a control that changes one thing and holds the rest fixed.

The strategy scores the same on real NSE data as on data with every trace of structure removed. Whatever it is reading, it is not the market.
07 — The entry, on its ownno exit rule involved

A band touch predicts nothing

The Monte Carlo is confirmed independently by a test that ignores the exit rule entirely. Take every bar where price touched an outer band, and measure the return over the next 5, 10 and 20 bars in the direction the fade is betting on, against the instrument's own unconditional drift.

events per cell   ~26,000
excess return     −0.03%  to  +0.08%
t-statistic       −0.58   to  +0.15
instruments positive     7 of 17

Zero, at every horizon, on both sides, with and without the gate. There is no predictive content in a band touch on these series. That is why no exit rule rescues it — and it is exactly what the 0-of-120 parameter grid and the 0-of-102 stop grid were already saying.

08 — What this does and doesn't say

Reading it honestly

  • It does not say the indicators are wrong. The blue-line gate helps. The band level is worth ~2.2 Sharpe as an execution reference. Both are real; both are small.
  • It does say the shape loses. A fixed 1.8 σ target against an unbounded loss needs an entry that is right roughly three times in four. This entry is right two times in three, and that gap is not closable by tuning.
  • The breakout mirror is deliberately not reported. Negating the position is a clean mirror only for close-to-close P&L — there it flips −0.32 to +0.32 gross, which still does not clear 2.3 bps of cost. With resting orders it is a different strategy needing its own exit rule, and inventing one would mean reporting a strategy nobody specified.
  • Stops were tested anyway, against the spec, because the loss shape invited it. They make it worse: −2.36 at a 1 σ stop against −1.34 with none. Your "no stop loss" instinct was the better of the two.

Where an edge could actually come from

The diagnosis points somewhere specific. The entry needs predictive content it currently has none of — a condition that separates the touches that revert from the touches that run. The gate is the right kind of idea and it is measurably worth something; it is simply far too weak on its own. The next honest test is to find a conditioner with a real t-statistic on that forward return before building any exit around it.
