Does the channel switch have an edge?

The idea: let a regression channel say whether the market is trending or ranging — fade the bands inside it, follow the 20-SMA when it breaks out. Four passes and twenty test stages later, half of it is dead, the other half has alpha, and the alpha is in the wrong hemisphere.

Half of it

The trend-following half is dead three ways over. The fading half does carry real alpha — but only on US equity indices, because those specific series mean-revert and most things do not. On NIFTY and BANK NIFTY, the instruments this system is built to trade, its alpha is significantly negative.

What was tested

The rule, stated precisely enough to be wrong

A rolling OLS fit of close on bar index over the last n bars gives a centre line and a residual sigma. z is where price sits in that channel; smaz is where the 20-SMA sits.

  • Range regime — the SMA is inside the ±kσ band. Go long when z ≤ −0.9k, short when z ≥ 0.9k, exit at the centre line.
  • Trend regime — the SMA breaks the band. Long above the upper band, short below the lower, flat again when it re-enters.
  • Defaults n=100, k=2.0, sma=20. Signal formed on bar t's close, filled at t+1, close-to-close P&L, ±1 unit, 5 bps per side. The code asserts the realised position is exactly the signal lagged one bar, and that truncating the future never changes a past value.
Read this before the numbers

This ran on the S&P 500, Nasdaq, WTI and GOOG daily plus EUR/USD hourly — not on NIFTY or BANKNIFTY. Every market-data host is blocked from this sandbox, and the repo's own downloader silently falls back to synthetic GBM, on which any timing rule is guaranteed to lose. So the history here comes from real observed prices bundled inside PyPI packages. The conclusions are about whether this class of rule works on real price series; the Indian read has to be re-run before it decides anything about capital.

Scoreboard

One win in five, on the friendliest instrument

Net-of-cost Sharpe, full sample, default parameters. The strategy is flat roughly half the time, so a lower Sharpe than buy & hold at half the exposure would still be interesting — but four of five are worse on both counts, and the fifth is inside the noise band established further down.

Test 1 · Structure

The switch never switches

A 20-bar average of price cannot deviate from a 100-bar regression line as far as price itself does — smoothing shrinks the deviation by roughly half. That is arithmetic, not a market fact, and it means the breakout condition is close to unreachable.

Which makes the "regime-switching strategy" a mean-reversion strategy with a rounding error attached. Turning the trend leg off changes almost nothing:

Test 3 · Significance

It does not clear its own null

Flip the sign of each daily return at random and re-run the whole strategy, 500 times. That keeps the volatility clustering the channel reacts to and destroys only the direction — so whatever Sharpe survives is what the machinery produces on structureless data.

Test 4 · Robustness

Tuning it would not rescue it

648 parameter combinations per instrument — lookback, band width, MA length, entry threshold, exit level. The question is not whether some combination looks good. Something always does. The question is whether the best one beats the best of 648 coin flips.

InstrumentBest of 648MedianDeflated SharpePBOWalk-forward OOSVerdict

PBO is the probability that the in-sample winner lands below median out of sample, measured across 924 combinatorial splits. Walk-forward picks the best config on everything seen so far and trades the next fold, five times over.

A trap worth naming

EUR/USD shows PBO of 0.05, which looks excellent until you notice why: every configuration is consistently terrible, so the in-sample winner reliably stays a winner among losers. A low PBO on a negative-Sharpe grid is not a green light — always read it next to the level.

Test 6 · The indicator file

The chart was filtering on something else entirely

Tests 1–5 took the hypothesis from its prose description. The actual Pine file changes the answer, because the chart carries two indicators and the regime variable is in the one I had not seen.

It settles the open parameters outright. The Bollinger block is display only — its own comment reads "This is PURELY VISUAL. It feeds nothing: not the direction features, not the intensity gate, not the regime label." Basis is SMA(close, 20), one shared ta.stdev (population), inner pair at 0.3σ as set on the chart, outer pair at 2.0σ. So the outer band is the cap — and 2.0 lands inside the 1.25–2.0 plateau the sweep had already found on its own.

The other half of the file is a five-label regime detector: a direction axis (softmax over five signed momentum features, with a confidence override and a confirmation delay) and an intensity axis (trend_z, Kaufman efficiency, a volatility cap). On S&P 500 daily it sits in SIDEWAYS 49.0% of the time, L_BULL 20.4%, L_BEAR 16.9%, H_BULL 8.1%, H_BEAR 5.7%.

Trading its labels loses money — exactly as its author warns

The file says: "NOT A SIGNAL... the 5-label forward-return ordering is BROKEN at every horizon. Do not treat H_BULL as 'go long'." That reproduces cleanly. Every spec containing a trend leg is negative on every instrument, which is the third independent way this hypothesis's trend half has failed — first it could not fire, then it lost money when forced to, now the purpose-built detector loses money too.

As a gate on fading, it produces real alpha

Near-zero beta, both directions profitable, positive in all four S&P sub-periods (+1.04 / +0.33 / +0.93 / +0.59), still +0.45 at 50 bps, and 95% of an 80-point parameter surface positive. v1's walk-forward was zero-to-negative on four of five instruments; here it is +0.58 and +0.49.

But on the S&P, the detector is not what earns it

The sharpest available control: rotate the label series by a random offset. Occupancy and run-length structure survive untouched; only the alignment to price is destroyed. If the detector's timing matters, the real labels must beat the rotated ones.

So the S&P number should be attributed to the fade plus any sparse, blocky entry filter — what the gate really does there is stop the fade re-entering over and over inside one persistent move. On the Nasdaq the detector's timing is doing genuine work.

And it uses one knob of the detector, not the detector

SIDEWAYS is decided on the direction axis alone. Under this spec, changing Z_HI, changing EFF_HI, or switching the volatility cap off — the file's headline "Deviation 2" — move the result by exactly zero. CONF_L is the single live knob, and 0.45–0.55 is a plateau (S&P +0.62 / +0.73 / +0.68). If gating a fade is the use case, the intensity machinery is not earning its complexity.

Test 7 · NIFTY, and the reason

It does not transfer, and the variance ratio says why

The spec that scored +0.73 on the S&P was run unchanged on NIFTY 50, BANK NIFTY, NIFTY 500 and fifteen major constituents — 2012–2026 for the indices, back to 1995 for the stocks. It does not work.

InstrumentStrategyBuy & holdAlpha t (NW)

Positive on 9 of 18. One stock clears t > 1.96 — which is exactly what fifteen independent tests hand you by chance. NIFTY 50's alpha is significantly negative.

The one number that predicts everything

The variance ratio VR(20) = Var(20-day return) / (20 × Var(1-day return)). Below 1, multi-day moves are smaller than a random walk implies — the series reverts, and fading a band is a bet that pays. Above 1, it trends, and the same bet is backwards. Plotted against what the strategy actually earned on each of the 23 series:

The event study is the strategy stripped of everything else: after price closes below the DevLucem lower band, what do the next five days do relative to the unconditional drift? US indices: +0.40%, t = +2.43. Indian indices: −0.21%, and in 2019–2026 alone −0.62% at t = −2.09. NIFTY continues after a band break. Fading it is the wrong side of a real effect.

The popular explanation is wrong

Index mean-reversion is usually explained by the leverage effect: panics spike volatility and the fade sells the variance premium. That story does not survive measurement — leverage correlation is −0.152 for US indices and −0.144 for Indian ones, essentially identical. India has the same vol-return asymmetry and none of the reversion. What actually differs is the bounce: the five-day return after a 1-sd down day minus that after a 1-sd up day is +0.48% in US indices and −0.17% in Indian ones. US dips get bought. Indian dips keep going.

And it is the market, not the period

The obvious objection is that the US sample runs 1999–2018 and the Indian one 2012–2026. Restricting both to the seven years they share:

2012–2018 onlyVR(20)Lower-break tStrategy Sharpe

Same seven years, opposite behaviour. The split is between markets, not between periods.

Where to go next

What is worth carrying forward

1. Stop trying to trade the trend half

Three independent tests have now killed it: the 20-SMA breakout is mechanically incapable of firing, forcing it to fire with a price trigger loses money, and the purpose-built regime detector's own high-intensity labels lose money in both directions on all five instruments. The detector's author already documented this. Treat H_BULL / H_BEAR as context for sizing or for standing aside — never as a direction.

2. The fade is the asset, and it needs a sparse entry filter

Against sign-flipped prices the DevLucem 2σ fade clears its null on both indices. Ungated it scores +0.375; gated to SIDEWAYS with the 2.0σ cap it scores +0.733 at beta 0.048. On the S&P a rotated label series gets most of the way there, so the mechanism to test next is not "which regime" but "how sparse, and how blocky" — a cooldown after each exit is the cheapest hypothesis, and it would be a fairer baseline than a random rotation.

3. For NIFTY, the sign is the finding

Positive AC(1), VR(20) above 1, and a significantly negative lower-band excess in 2019–2026 all say the same thing: Indian indices continue after a band break. That is a trend-following setup on the very same indicator — the exact opposite of the fade, and the version worth building next. The trend leg failed here because it was tested on series that revert; on NIFTY it would be tested on series that trend.

3b. Screen instruments before building on them

VR(20) and the band-break event study cost nothing and run on any candidate before a line of strategy code exists. Anything with VR(20) ≥ 1 should never see a fade. Both are in mechanism.py.

4. Keep the gates that caught the first two rounds

PBO, deflated Sharpe, sign-flip nulls, rotated-label controls and anchored walk-forward are cheap and they are what separated the real result from the two that looked plausible and were not. Worth wiring into seeded_edges/revalidate_edges.py as a gate every candidate must clear before it reaches the shadow ledger — with the rotated-label control as the standing requirement for anything claiming a regime filter helps.
