Does the channel switch have an edge?

The idea: let a regression channel say whether the market is trending or ranging — fade the bands inside it, follow the 20-SMA when it breaks out. Nine tests later, here is what the data says.

Not an edge

No alpha found — and the rule has not been overfitted yet, because there is nothing here to overfit. The trend half never fires, the half that does is long beta, and it does not clear its own null.

Trend leg trigger rate 0.07–0.73% of bars

The 20-SMA breakout fires 2–3 times in twenty years, and cannot fire at all once k ≥ 2.5.

Best alpha t-stat 1.26

Newey-West, S&P 500, after stripping out market beta of 0.26. Nothing reaches 1.96.

Best deflated Sharpe 0.74

Probability the best of 648 tuned configs beats the best of 648 coin flips. Needs 0.95.

What was tested

The rule, stated precisely enough to be wrong

A rolling OLS fit of close on bar index over the last n bars gives a centre line and a residual sigma. z is where price sits in that channel; smaz is where the 20-SMA sits.

  • Range regime — the SMA is inside the ±kσ band. Go long when z ≤ −0.9k, short when z ≥ 0.9k, exit at the centre line.
  • Trend regime — the SMA breaks the band. Long above the upper band, short below the lower, flat again when it re-enters.
  • Defaults n=100, k=2.0, sma=20. Signal formed on bar t's close, filled at t+1, close-to-close P&L, ±1 unit, 5 bps per side. The code asserts the realised position is exactly the signal lagged one bar, and that truncating the future never changes a past value.
Read this before the numbers

This ran on the S&P 500, Nasdaq, WTI and GOOG daily plus EUR/USD hourly — not on NIFTY or BANKNIFTY. Every market-data host is blocked from this sandbox, and the repo's own downloader silently falls back to synthetic GBM, on which any timing rule is guaranteed to lose. So the history here comes from real observed prices bundled inside PyPI packages. The conclusions are about whether this class of rule works on real price series; the Indian read has to be re-run before it decides anything about capital.

Scoreboard

One win in five, on the friendliest instrument

Net-of-cost Sharpe, full sample, default parameters. The strategy is flat roughly half the time, so a lower Sharpe than buy & hold at half the exposure would still be interesting — but four of five are worse on both counts, and the fifth is inside the noise band established further down.

Test 1 · Structure

The switch never switches

A 20-bar average of price cannot deviate from a 100-bar regression line as far as price itself does — smoothing shrinks the deviation by roughly half. That is arithmetic, not a market fact, and it means the breakout condition is close to unreachable.

Which makes the "regime-switching strategy" a mean-reversion strategy with a rounding error attached. Turning the trend leg off changes almost nothing:

What is left is long beta

Test 3 · Significance

It does not clear its own null

Flip the sign of each daily return at random and re-run the whole strategy, 500 times. That keeps the volatility clustering the channel reacts to and destroys only the direction — so whatever Sharpe survives is what the machinery produces on structureless data.

Test 4 · Robustness

Tuning it would not rescue it

648 parameter combinations per instrument — lookback, band width, MA length, entry threshold, exit level. The question is not whether some combination looks good. Something always does. The question is whether the best one beats the best of 648 coin flips.

InstrumentBest of 648MedianDeflated SharpePBOWalk-forward OOSVerdict

PBO is the probability that the in-sample winner lands below median out of sample, measured across 924 combinatorial splits. Walk-forward picks the best config on everything seen so far and trades the next fold, five times over.

A trap worth naming

EUR/USD shows PBO of 0.05, which looks excellent until you notice why: every configuration is consistently terrible, so the in-sample winner reliably stays a winner among losers. A low PBO on a negative-Sharpe grid is not a green light — always read it next to the level.

Where to go next

Two structural lessons worth keeping

1. The regime variable is wrong, not just badly tuned

An SMA-versus-band breakout compares a smoothed quantity to a band built from unsmoothed residuals. It is the wrong dimensionless number. The channel already carries its own read on how directional the market is: slope × n / sigma — the drift across the window measured in units of noise, which is the fit's R² in another costume. That is a real regime variable with a real distribution. slope_max and slope_min_trend in Params implement it; variants F–H are a first cut and are still negative, but they at least test the intended idea rather than an unreachable one.

2. Fading a band is directionally biased by construction

In any series with drift, the long side inherits the drift and the short side fights it. That guarantees a long-biased P&L that looks like skill in a bull market. Score the next version on beta-adjusted alpha with a Newey-West t-stat, not on raw Sharpe, or it will keep rediscovering the equity risk premium and charging you costs for the privilege.

3. Set the bar before the search, not after

The machinery in this repo — PBO, deflated Sharpe, sign-flip nulls, anchored walk-forward — is cheap to run and is what separates an edge from a fitted curve. Worth wiring into seeded_edges/revalidate_edges.py as a gate every candidate has to pass before it reaches the shadow ledger: deflated Sharpe ≥ 0.95, PBO ≤ 0.3, walk-forward OOS Sharpe within a stated fraction of in-sample, and an alpha t-stat that clears 2 after beta.

And re-run all of it on NIFTY, BANKNIFTY and the MCX contracts before any of it counts.
