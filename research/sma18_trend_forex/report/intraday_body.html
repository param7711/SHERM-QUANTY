Liquidity vs. result — 15m, 30m and 1h only

Same rank correlation as the daily liquidity test above (median daily notional USD traded, excluding the six commodities with broken Yahoo volume), computed instead against the 15m, 30m and 1h backtests. This is the timeframe range where the liquidity story should matter most, if it matters at all: a rule trading many times a day is far more exposed to spread and depth than one holding for a week.

Why this is a weaker test than the daily one — read before the numbers

  • 15m and 30m carry ~60 calendar days of data; 1h carries ~730 (yfinance hard caps, same limit noted in the grid section). A Sharpe computed on 60 days is extremely noisy, and none of these three timeframes has a Markov p-value behind it — there isn't enough history to fit a null model to. This section correlates liquidity against a noisy point estimate, not against an established edge the way the daily section does.
  • Liquidity itself is measured from daily volume, not from anything at 15m/30m/1h. It's a reasonable stand-in for how deep an instrument generally is, but it says nothing about intraday spread specifically.
  • Same six commodities excluded for broken volume data (SILVER, PLATINUM, PALLADIUM, COPPER, COFFEE, COTTON) — leaving 10 commodities and 14 crypto, small enough that one instrument can move a correlation materially.
